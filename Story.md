## Premise

The player is a newly appointed government official in the Republic of Vlastok, assigned to the **Ministry of Documentation**. The Ministry frames the documentation, paper trails and workflows of the state: the procedures, authentication and certification of every national document, such as IDs, wills, and decisions of the Party and the state in general.

Over the course of the game the player rises through the Ministry and uncovers the Truth behind each of the Party's celebrated reforms, ultimately learning that the Ministry of Documentation is, in reality, the **Ministry of Propaganda**.

- [ ] **EXPAND:** Core gameplay loop. What does a single in-game workday look like? Documents received, tools available, quotas, time limits, and the consequences of mistakes.

## Setting

### The Republic of Vlastok
Vlastok is a republic that has been governed by a single party for a century. Its two neighboring nations are, in practice, Vlastok's puppet states (see *Peace and Foreign Relations*).

### Era and Architecture
The game is set in the **1960s–1970s**. Vlastok is dominated by large Soviet-style structures. The city has been planned with wide streets for monumental buildings and to give military vehicles easy access to the State's key points of interest.

- [ ] **EXPAND:** Technology level of the Ministry (typewriters, rubber stamps, carbon copies, microfilm, punch-card ledgers). This defines how document editing works as a mechanic.

## History

### The War Years
A century ago, Vlastok was a war-ridden state. Its citizens died of starvation, malnutrition, sickness and war, while the national treasury was constantly in debt. The two political factions representing the general populace, the **Blue Party** and the **Red Party**, were also at war with each other.

### The Founding of the Purple Party
Following a period of civil unrest, the leader of the Blue Party was found dead amid the chaos. Out of respect, and as a way of honoring his political adversary, the leader of the Red Party resigned from his position. Members of the two parties, with the blessing of the people, founded the **Purple Party (PP)** to show that this was a new party and a new start in what would be a peaceful and prosperous era for Vlastok.

- [ ] **EXPAND:** Who actually killed the Blue Party leader? A strong late-game Truth candidate: was his death staged by the founders of the PP?
- [ ] **EXPAND:** What happened to the Red Party leader after his resignation?

### The Centennial
This year marks the 100th anniversary of the PP. Vlastok will hold a celebration, and the Purple Party will be renamed the **Grand Public Party (Grand PP)**. In preparation, the Ministry of Documentation has taken on new hires, among them the player.

> **CONSISTENCY NOTE:** The original draft says the Grand Party "won the last elections 100 years ago", but also describes the party as founded by consensus after civil unrest. Decide whether the PP came to power by election, by consensus, or both, and whether any elections have been held since.

- [ ] **EXPAND:** The centennial celebration as a set piece: a parade, a hard document deadline, or the game's climactic event.

## The Purple Party's Reforms: Propaganda vs. Truth

Each reform of the PP has an official public version (**Propaganda**) and a hidden reality (**Truth**). The player gradually uncovers each Truth through the documents he processes, so each reform doubles as a gameplay chapter with its own document types.

| Reform | Official story (Propaganda) | Documents the player handles |
|---|---|---|
| Abolition of physical currency | Protects citizens from theft | Financial and income statements, national payment receipts, wills |
| Universal healthcare | Free care and vaccines for all | Medical records, death certificates, vaccination certificates |
| Peace and foreign relations | No wars; friendly neighbors | Citizenship papers, residency permits, wills, census data |
| The free press | Free media, no party bias | Newspapers, film and TV scripts |
| Religion | Private faith prevents conflict | Books, letters, conversation transcripts |

### Abolition of Physical Currency
- **Propaganda:** Due to poverty and famine, many people were stealing money. Abolishing physical currency and keeping each person's finances in banks was presented as a move that increased the security of the citizens.
- **Truth:** With all currency held in banks and every citizen's wage recorded in a ledger, the Party could use the money any way it saw fit: aid to allies and allied nations, personal riches, weapons expenditure, and R&D for weapons and genetic modification. Citizens could still use the money they needed, whenever possible.
- **Documents the player handles:** financial statements, income statements, receipts of payment to the nation, wills.
- [ ] **EXPAND:** A named citizen whose ledger the player alters, and whose consequences the player later witnesses.

### Universal Healthcare
- **Propaganda:** In the years after the war, many illnesses plagued the nation. The PP made sure to give everyone access to healthcare and vaccinations, regardless of social or economic background.
- **Truth:** In some cases the PP spread false news of infectious outbreaks in order to conduct genetic experiments or test chemical weapons. Citizens were also used for transplants, blood-bag stockpiles, organ sales and anything else that could empower and enrich the state and the PP.
- **Documents the player handles:** medical records, proofs of death, vaccination certificates.
- [ ] **EXPAND:** Link this to the genetic-modification R&D funded through the currency reform. What is the program's goal, and does the player ever see its results?

### Peace and Foreign Relations
- **Propaganda:** No wars have happened since the PP's founding. Diplomatic relations with other nations improved, and Vlastok's immediate neighbors became positive toward it.
- **Truth:** Vlastok funded both of its neighbors to continue hostilities against each other, and was paid not in money but in the materials and resources needed for its own stability. Once the war had exhausted both nations, Vlastok seized the opportunity and turned them into puppet states.
- **Documents the player handles:** citizenship papers (converting citizens of the neighboring nations to Vlastok's), wills, residency permits, national census data.
- [ ] **EXPAND:** Name the two neighboring nations and define their cultures, languages and current status.

### The Free Press
- **Propaganda:** To stop the Blue and Red Parties' influence on the local papers and dissolve the deep-seated rivalry between their supporters, the PP dissolved all newspaper, radio and TV companies and created three new ones that provide their services for free, ensuring the press no longer passes on the grievances of the political parties.
- **Truth:** Vlastok created a highly regulated press system through which it controls the flow of information and steers public opinion toward specific goals.
- **Documents the player handles:** newspapers, scripts for films and TV series.
- [ ] **EXPAND:** Name the three state media outlets and give each a distinct tone and audience.

### Religion
- **Propaganda:** Every citizen has the right to fulfill his religious duties, as long as there are no temples, no group gatherings, and no mention of specific gods or belief systems. This ensures the populace has no extra reasons to enter into ideological conflict.
- **Truth:** Abolishing the Church made the State the sole provider of hope.
- **Documents the player handles:** books, letters and conversations on religious topics.
- [ ] **EXPAND:** What faith(s) existed before the PP? Is there an underground movement the player could encounter, expose or protect?

## Characters

### The Player
The only child of Katriona and Basil. After graduating law school, he is contacted by the Ministry and offered the position of Validity Officer.
- [ ] **EXPAND:** Name, personality and appearance. Does the player's law background unlock special abilities, dialogue options or document insights?

### Katriona and Basil
The player's parents: a low-income family who spent all their savings so their son could graduate law school.
- [ ] **EXPAND:** Their role during the game. Do their documents ever cross the player's desk? Could the Ministry use them as leverage?

### The Supervisor
The player's superior is only ever called "the Supervisor"; the player never learns his name. There is only one Supervisor in each ministry. Supervisors live in separate sectors of the city where they never come into contact with ordinary citizens, and they must wear a face mask at all times.

Officially, this is because the PP still remembers and honors the tragic death of the Blue Party's leader: should there ever be an uprising, the Supervisors will be safe.
- [ ] **EXPAND:** The Supervisor's arc and voice: how he communicates (written notes, intercom, in person) and what lies behind the mask. This ties directly into the "Become the next Supervisor" ending.

### Fellow New Hires
The player works alongside other new entries to the Ministry. Some are abruptly transferred to other offices or mysteriously decide to leave Vlastok. Some time later, the player must correct documents that contain their information and their story.
- [ ] **EXPAND:** Write 3–5 named coworkers, each with a fate tied to one of the reforms above.

## Career Progression

The game is structured around three ranks, each expanding the player's power over documents.

### Stage 1: Validity Officer
The player may only note discrepancies.

### Stage 2: Correction Officer
The player edits documents on paper, on demand.

### Stage 3: Ministry Accolade
When the player notes a discrepancy, he has the freedom to correct it, alter it, or edit it to fully match and display the correct and truthful depiction of the situation.

- [ ] **EXPAND:** Promotion triggers (performance, loyalty, number of days) and which Truth is revealed at each stage.
- [ ] **EXPAND:** A hidden morality or complicity score that tracks the player's choices and feeds into the endings.

## Endings

After all the Truths have been revealed, and the player learns that the Ministry of Documentation is in actuality the Ministry of Propaganda, he is presented with three choices:

1. **Escape:** flee the country toward a better future.
2. **Atonement:** end his life for all the harm he has caused.
3. **Succession:** become the next Supervisor.

- [ ] **EXPAND:** Conditions for each ending, and whether earlier choices (e.g. secretly restoring truthful documents as Ministry Accolade) unlock variations or a fourth ending.

## Glossary

- **Vlastok:** The republic in which the game takes place.
- **PP / Purple Party:** The ruling party, founded by members of the former Blue and Red Parties.
- **Grand PP / Grand Public Party:** The PP's new name from its 100th anniversary.
- **Ministry of Documentation:** The player's employer; secretly the Ministry of Propaganda.
- **Supervisor:** The single, nameless, masked head of each ministry.
- **Validity Officer:** Rank 1, notes discrepancies.
- **Correction Officer:** Rank 2, edits documents on demand.
- **Ministry Accolade:** Rank 3, free to correct, alter or edit any discrepancy.
